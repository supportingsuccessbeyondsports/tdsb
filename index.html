What the research found

Three themes came up again and again.

01

A Tough Balancing Act

Many students shared that managing both academics and sports is a real challenge. Between practices, games, and schoolwork, finding time to stay on top of everything often feels overwhelming — especially during busy seasons.

02

Lack of Teacher Support

Several participants felt teachers don't always understand the pressure of being a student-athlete. When sports commitments clash with school responsibilities, students said they often receive limited flexibility, which can affect their performance and confidence.

03

Mental Wellness Matters

While sports can be great for stress relief, the pressure to succeed in both academics and athletics at once can also take a toll on mental health.

The playbook

Five priorities, for educators and students alike.

For Educators

Time Management Support — Achieve

Provide clear schedules for due dates and deadlines, since athletes may be missing school for games or travel.

Teacher Flexibility — Belong

Offer flexible deadlines and real opportunities for students to catch up on missed work.

Balance Encouragement — Thrive

Stronger communication reduces stress and prevents misunderstandings between students and staff.

Stronger Communication — Revitalize

Regular check-ins and shared expectations help students stay connected to the support they need.

Mental Wellness Support — Thrive

A designated staff contact for check-ins can ease pressure and help students succeed in both worlds.

For Students

Time Management — Achieve

Use a calendar to track schoolwork and games together — it helps avoid last-minute scrambling.

Teacher Flexibility — Belong

Give teachers 2–3 days' notice before missing class for a sport, whenever possible.

Balance Encouragement — Thrive

Set clear goals so study time and practice time both get protected, not just whatever's left over.

Stronger Communication — Revitalize

Open up to teachers, coaches, and parents about challenges — support only helps if people know it's needed.

Mental Wellness — Thrive

Protect a good sleep schedule, take breaks, and make room for whatever genuinely relieves stress.

About the research

I'm Halima.

This site explores the impact of extracurricular sports on the academic success of Black students in grades 9–12 within the Toronto District School Board. It shares findings from my action research project, highlighting both the benefits and challenges of balancing sports with school.

From my own experience, I know participating in sports builds real skills — confidence, leadership, teamwork — but it can also bring academic challenges. Keeping up with schoolwork while protecting good mental health doesn't always come easy, and this project is about naming that honestly.
