The playbook

Recommendation/Tips

With all of the concerns that Black Student Athletes have reported, here are the best tips and recommendations to help them succeed. Each recommendation is connected to Toronto District School Board's Multi-Year Strategic Plan.

For Educators

Better Time Management Support — Achieve

Teachers can help by helping students with providing schedules for students when it comes to due dates and deadlines since athletes may be missing a due date/deadline.

Flexibility — Belong

Teachers can provide a flexibility deadline and opportunities for students to catch up on missed work.

Encourage Balance — Thrive

Teachers can encourage a healthy balance by checking in with students, helping them feel supported, easing stress, and strengthening communication between school and sport.

Stronger Communication — Revitalize

Better communication with students support team, and check ins/shared expectations will help students to stay more connected and can get the support they need.

Mental Wellness Support — Thrive

Schools providing a staff to check in with students can help the pressure go away and students can be capable of exceeding in both.

For Students

Better Time Management Support — Achieve

Using a calendar to stay on track with all of your work/games. This will help avoiding procrastination for students.

Flexibility — Belong

Students should communicate with their teachers 2-3 days in advance if they will miss a class due to a sport.

Encourage Balance — Thrive

Setting goals so they can use time to study and practice on their own time which can help students use their time effectively.

Stronger Communication — Revitalize

Students can open up with teachers, coaches and parents about any struggles or challenges if they are facing any.

Mental Wellness Support — Thrive

Students should prioritize their mental health by creating a good healthy sleep schedule, taking breaks when needed and doing self care or something that can make all their stress go away.

Taking care of yourself

Mental Wellness Tips & Strategies

Always prioritize your mental health - Your wellbeing is just as important as your performance in school and academics, taking care of yourself can help you succeed in both.

Getting Enough Sleep

Try to aim to sleep with 8-10 hours.

Taking care of your body

Eating healthy and balanced foods.

Taking breaks

Taking a break away from everything and taking deep breaths can help you recharge.

Building a support team

Building a support team and surrounding yourself with people who encourage you and support all of your pathways.

Practicing Self Care

Creating time to do things you enjoy and finding a routine to relax your mind.

Balancing sports and academics isn't always about being perfect, finding things that works best for you to help you succeed is what the most important part is. Whether that's setting goals or taking breaks, every student athlete works differently and their paths are a lot different. Most importantly, don't forget to ask for help from someone you trust is a key way to succeed.

Fuel for two full-time jobs

Nutrition & Fitness

Being a student-athlete is basically two jobs — your body and brain need real fuel to keep up with both.

Nutrition for Energy

Eating a mix of carbohydrates: such as Oatmeal, Whole Wheat Foods, Sweet Potatoes, etc.

Hydration & Recovery

  • Drinking water all day long, not just when you feel thirsty
  • Eat protein to help your muscles repair: Eggs, Chicken Breast, Salmon, Yogurt Tofu

Smart Training Habits

  • Cardio exercises to build a strong body
  • Change your routine often so you do not hurt your joints
  • Paying close attention to your body, taking rest days when you feel too tired

It's okay to not have it all figured out

Coping with Stress & Anxiety

Between tests, games, and everything in between, stress is going to show up. What matters is having healthy ways to move through it.

Before the Game

  • Your only job: Focus on your effort. You can't control the referee, weather or crowd.
  • Action goals: Set goals for your actions (like play aggressive).
  • Movie Mind: Imagine yourself making great plays in the upcoming match.

In the Game

  • Double Inhale: Breathe in twice quickly through your nose, then blow it out long through your mouth.
  • Stare Step: Picking one object in the stadium and stare at it for 5 seconds to focus and get your head back in the game.
  • Reset Act: Wiping your shoes to try to erase the last bad play and get it out your head.
  • Gear Fix: Spending time to fix up your laces to stop racing all negative thoughts.

After the Game

  • Good & Bad: Write down two things you did well, and two things that you can improve on for the next match.
  • Slow Stretch: Doing at least 5 minutes of slow stretching after the match so your body knows the game is over.
  • Q & A: Asking a teammate, coach, or parent how well you did and ask what mistake they noticed throughout the match.

If stress or anxiety is sticking around beyond game day, it's worth talking to a professional — a school counsellor, doctor, or a service like Kids Help Phone (see the Resources page). Asking for help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
